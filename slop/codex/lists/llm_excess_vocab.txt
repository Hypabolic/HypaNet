# slop/codex/lists/llm_excess_vocab.txt
# "Excess vocabulary": words whose frequency rose sharply after LLM chatbots became common, measured in large corpora
# (PubMed abstracts, conference peer reviews, Wikipedia edits). Pattern L04 ("delve"-class diction), L06 (era
# drift: the GPT-6 list in section C), and the phrase entries of L05 (significance puffery) and L18 (copula
# substitutes) taken from Wikipedia in section C; they feed the density rule of L01-L05.
#
# These lists were measured on scientific and encyclopaedic prose, not on UI copy. In app and site copy most of them
# are rare, so a hit is a strong but uncommon signal; many entries ("offers", "serves", "valuable") are ordinary words,
# and only density (several distinct entries in one node or screen) is meaningful. Wikipedia's advice page warns that
# overuse of a word does not imply overuse of its synonyms: do not expand these entries with a thesaurus.
#
# Format: one entry per line, lower case, as spelled in the source (mostly American spelling). Lines starting with #
# are comments. Section headers give the source of every entry below them. Each entry appears once, under the
# first section that names it; a later source that names the same word is not repeated (so Kobak et al. and
# Wikipedia also list most of section A).
#
# Provenance: each section header below names the source of every entry under it.
# Licence: this file mixes entries from MIT-licensed data (Kobak et al.), facts reported in papers (Juzek & Ward;
# Liang et al.; Reinhart et al.) and word lists from Wikipedia (CC BY-SA 4.0). To respect the share-alike terms of the

# and URLs: SOURCES.md.
